Produced by a read-only multi-agent review of the full text of all 26 papers in `704papers/` (three themed readers + a synthesizer/critic), 2026-10-15. **Status is as reviewed against v2**; the v3 work that closed items is listed first. Experiments and evidence are abbreviated; see `dataset/docs/V3_DETECTOR.md` for results.

Totals at review time: {'PARTIAL': 38, 'APPLICABLE': 40, 'NOT_APPLICABLE': 34, 'APPLIED': 17}.

## What can honestly be claimed

WHAT CAN BE CLAIMED ABOUT '100% TPR' 1. No static detector can guarantee 100% TPR on families it has never seen.    - Stream of Problems: "an ML model is a type of signature"; there is no 0-day detector.    - Humans vs Machines: the best ML player reached about 94% TPR at 1% FPR, even under random CV.    - EMBER2024 SOTA is about 94.5% TPR at 1% FPR on a temporal split.    - The only honest target is to maximize held-out-family TPR at a native-EXE FPR bound, with CIs. 2. On the visible 50/50 set, even a perfect 50/50 has a Wilson 95% lower bound of only 92.9%. Today's 48/50 is [86.5%, 98.9%], and 0/50 FP has an upper bound of 7.1%. 3. That set has already been used for selection. ensemble_stack.json records T moved from the calibrated 2.688 to 1.80 because 1.80 passes the 50/50 set. So 48/50 is a post-selection number, not an estimate. Any further tuning that reaches 50/50 on it is test-set fitting and must not be presented as a TPR improvement.  WHAT THE CURRENT NUMBERS MEAN (verified read-only) - The 83.6% 'recent TPR' is on a family-stratified split, so it covers known families. - 1,021 of the 1,455 live malware are rf_adv training rows, so cached stack scores on live malware are partly in-sample: 90.5% vs 83.6% held-out. - '<1% FPR' holds only for the .NET-DLL-dominated corpus mix:   - Deployed native-EXE FPR is 13/339 = 3.8% (Wilson 2.3-6.5%) in-corpus.   - It is 10/116 = 8.6% (Wilson 4.7-15.1%) on the new independent local corpus.   - On a hidden goodware set of diverse native apps, one FP in 50 already fails the bar. Sysinternals (signed MSVC Microsoft tools) is a comparatively easy case. - 26 of the 42 corpus FPs, and 902 of 914 local flags, are a features.py:35 crash (X1), not model behaviour. - 85 of the 168 in-sample misses are within 1 logit of T. Lowering T to chase the last grading samples trades directly against native-EXE FPR, which is already above 1% on independent data.  READER OVER-OPTIMISM CORRECTED - The 617-dim byte-slice model, rated 'potentially the largest gain', already measured weak OOF: 14% TPR at native-EXE 1% FPR (e2_quick). - The E4 fine-tune is positive but recreates the wmplayer.exe FP at all-goodware calibration (2/7 course EXEs flagged). - Signature-integrity gains are concentrated in GuLoader (36/95) and Vidar (26/66). That is weak evidence for unseen-family transfer. - My logit-fusion diagnostic (+3.4 pts) used in-sample thresholds and two hand-picked combinations, so it is mildly optimistic.
